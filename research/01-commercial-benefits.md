# Cooum: commercial benefits of cleaning (Research track 1)

_Last updated 24 Nov 2026. All ₹ figures in crore (1 cr = ₹10M). FX assumption ₹85/US$._

## Bottom line

A clean Cooum is worth roughly **₹12,600 to ₹29,400 cr in one-time property value uplift** plus **₹190 to ₹825 cr a year in recurring benefits** (flood losses avoided, health costs avoided, riverfront spending). Against a restoration cost of **₹735 to ₹2,400 cr**, the case pays back many times over, but only if the city captures part of the land value gain. Most of the upside lands in private hands as property value. Without a value capture mechanism, the government pays and owners collect.

## 1. Baseline facts

- Cooum runs about 65 km to the Bay of Bengal, about **20 km inside Chennai**. The restoration stretch is 32 km, from Paruthipattu check dam to the mouth. ([CRRT](https://crrt.tn.gov.in/cooum_restoration))
- Cost figures vary by scope and year: **₹735.08 cr** administrative sanction for the Integrated Cooum River Eco-Restoration Project ([CRRT](https://crrt.tn.gov.in/cooum_restoration)); **₹1,650 cr** across 69 sub-projects in three phases ([Citizen Matters, 2017](https://citizenmatters.in/?p=19711)); a 2019 headline of **₹2,371 cr** for Chennai's rivers overall ([Deccan Chronicle](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/nation/current-affairs/120719/rs-2371-crore-will-be-spent-to-restore-chennai-rivers-cm.html)).
- The **2015 Chennai floods** caused about **US$2.2 bn (~₹18,700 cr) in economic loss**, with US$755M insured. Swiss Re via [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/news-ians/chennai-floods-caused-economic-loss-of-2-2-bn-study-116033000930_1.html).
- Inadequate sanitation cost India **6.4% of GDP** (US$53.8 bn, US$48 a head) in 2006. 72% of that was health. The poorest urban fifth bear 75% more than the national average. ([World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2010/12/20/inadequate-sanitation-costs-india-the-equivalent-of-64-per-cent-of-gdp))
- Property prices near the river: Chennai city averages about ₹9,200/sq ft; Choolai is ₹8,900, Egmore ₹16,750 and Nungambakkam ₹15,750 ([SquareYards, Sep 2026](https://www.squareyards.com/property-rates/choolai-chennai)). Chetpet is listed at ₹16,000 to ₹24,000 ([Housing.com](https://www.housing.com/price-trends/property-rates-for-buy-in-chetpet_chennai-P6r6aaxy9u8zccmai)). These listing sites contradict themselves, so treat them as rough.

## 2. What cleaned rivers delivered elsewhere

| River | Cost | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| **Cheonggyecheon, Seoul** (2005, 5.8 km) | ~US$380M | Land prices **up 30 to 50% within 50 m**, double the Seoul rate. ~US$1.98 bn of area investment (about 5x the cost). 64,000 visitors a day. Businesses grew at twice the downtown rate. Area 3.3 to 5.9°C cooler, fine dust down 35%, flood protection to a 200-year event. ([Landscape Performance Series](https://www.landscapeperformance.org/case-study-briefs/cheonggyecheon-stream-restoration)) |
| **Singapore River** (1977 to 1987) | S$200 to 300M | 26,000 families rehoused, 4,926 hawkers moved to food centres, 610 pig farms and 500 duck farms phased out. The cost ended up about 10x the 1950s estimate because of delay. The river became the base for Boat Quay and Clarke Quay. ([LKY School](https://lkyspp.nus.edu.sg/gia/article/clean-up-of-the-singapore-river-before-and-after)) |
| **Sabarmati, Ahmedabad** (2005 to 2012) | ~₹1,400 cr spent | 203 ha reclaimed in phase 1. 14% was set aside for sale to fund the project, with ₹6,000 cr expected. **The 2017 auction failed, and the plots were still unsold in 2023.** About 786 MLD of untreated sewage still reaches the river. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabarmati_Riverfront), [Business Standard](https://business-standard.com/article/economy-policy/once-a-dry-river-sabarmati-may-now-garner-rs-6-000-cr-for-hotels-malls-117051700876_1.html)) |

**Lessons for Cooum**
1. Water quality comes before the promenade. Sabarmati built the riverfront without fixing sewage, so the river is still polluted.
2. Selling land is a weak way to pay for the project, because markets stall. Taxing the value gain is more reliable: a betterment levy, paid FSI premium or TDR on the uplift zone.
3. Delay compounds cost. Singapore's bill grew 10x.
4. Treat resettlement as part of the project budget. It was about half of Sabarmati's cost and the largest piece of Singapore's work.

## 3. How each estimate was built (adjustable)

**Property uplift.** The model uses 20 km of city river.
- Band A covers 0 to 100 m from each bank, about 4 sq km. That is roughly 43M sq ft of floor area at ₹10,000/sq ft, with an uplift of 15 to 30%. Seoul saw 30 to 50%. The model uses a lower figure because land titles are messier here and the cleanup will be gradual.
- Band B covers 100 to 500 m, about 16 sq km. That is roughly 172M sq ft at ₹12,000, with an uplift of 3 to 8%.
- Both bands assume built floor area roughly equal to land area once roads and open space are netted out. **Verify this against GCC property tax data.**

**Flood losses avoided.** A 2015-scale event costs ₹18,700 cr and happens about once in 15 years. Adding smaller annual floods gives an expected Chennai flood loss of ₹1,500 to 2,500 cr a year. The Cooum basin's share is taken as 15 to 25%. Desilting, opening the river mouth and clearing encroachments then cut that share by 20 to 40%.

**Health costs avoided.** About 1.0 to 1.5M people live within about 1 km of the river (an assumption). The 2006 sanitation cost of ₹2,180 a head is inflated to about ₹7,000 in 2026. 10 to 20% of that is attributed to the open sewer. This does not count dengue and malaria from stagnant water.

**Riverfront spending.** 20,000 to 50,000 visitors a day spend ₹100 to 200 each. Seoul gets 64,000 a day on a shorter stream. Chennai has a metro population of about 10M and few public green spaces.

**Not costed:** a heat-island cooling effect of 3 to 6°C (lower AC loads), carbon from mangroves at the estuary, ferry or water-taxi use (low, since bridge clearances and depth limit it), estuary fisheries (small), and lower municipal costs for repeated desilting and waste clean-up.

## 5. Revenue model: free walk, water travel, leased shops

Kid's chosen model: **walking is free**; money comes from **leased shops on developed government land** (riverbanks, entrances, small connecting bridges) and **water travel**.

### Comparables
- **Kochi Water Metro**: cost ₹1,137 cr for 76 km and 38 terminals, with fares of ₹20 to 40. Ridership is about 7,000 a day, against the 8,000 a day it needs to break even. It earns about ₹2.5 lakh a day and has run at a loss for two years. It is now adding terminal kiosks, space leasing and in-boat branding to make money beyond fares. ([The Week, Jan 2026](https://www.theweek.in/news/maritime/2026/01/15/kochi-water-metro-aims-to-break-even-by-mid-2026-all-you-need-to-know.html), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kochi_Water_Metro))
- **Akshar River Cruise, Sabarmati**: a private operator put in ₹10 cr and pays the riverfront company a **₹45 lakh/yr licence fee**. Tickets are ₹2,124 to 2,360 for a 1.5-hour meal cruise with 125 to 150 seats. ([DeshGujarat](https://deshgujarat.com/2023/07/10/akshar-river-cruise-restaurant-in-ahmedabad-booking-process-timings-prices-and-menu-detail))
- **Marina Beach smart carts (Chennai)**: 900 carts, with vendors paying ₹1,200 a month plus a ₹1,500 to 3,000 annual licence. This is a livelihood rate and sets the floor for kiosk pricing. ([DT Next, 2020](https://www.dtnext.in/news/chennai/rs-1200month-to-be-charged-from-vendors-for-smart-cart))
- **Chennai retail**: high streets account for 83% of leasing, with F&B at 19%. Rents rose 2.1% in a quarter and vacancy is 9.5% ([JLL Q2 2026](https://research.jllapsites.com/appd-market-report/q2-2026-retail-chennai/)). JLL publishes no per-sq-ft rent, so the shop rents below are an assumption to verify with local brokers.

### Revenue estimate (₹ cr per year, 20 km city stretch)

| Stream | Assumption | Low | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kiosks on the promenade | 400 kiosks of about 25 sq ft at ₹3,000 to 8,000 a month | 1.4 | 3.8 |
| Small shops at entrances and bridges | 20 entrances × 10 shops plus 15 bridges × 6 shops, which is 290 shops of about 200 sq ft, at ₹60 to 150 per sq ft a month | 4.2 | 10.4 |
| Anchor cafés and restaurants | 10 sites on Akshar-style licences of ₹30 to 60 lakh a year | 3.0 | 6.0 |
| Ads, events, parking | Lump estimate | 2.0 | 5.0 |
| Water travel fares | 10 electric boats on 8 to 12 navigable km, 2,000 to 6,000 riders a day at about ₹50 | 3.7 | 11.0 |
| Cruise concessions | 1 or 2 licences at about ₹45 lakh | 0.5 | 0.9 |
| **Total** | | **~15** | **~37** |

### What this means
1. **Shop and boat revenue can pay for running the riverfront, but it cannot pay back the build.** ₹15 to 37 cr a year is enough to cover operations and maintenance: robots, monitoring, cleaning crews, boats. At that rate, recovering the ₹735 to 2,400 cr build would take 20 to 160 years. The capital has to come from government grants and from capturing the property value gain in section 3.
2. **Treat water travel as a way to draw visitors, not a profit centre.** Even Kochi, at much larger scale, struggles to cover operating costs from fares. Run tourist trips at a higher fare (₹100 to 200) and commuter trips at Kochi-level fares (₹20 to 40). Bring in a private cruise operator under a licence so the operator carries the boat cost.
3. **Free entry is what makes the shop rents work.** Footfall is what tenants pay for. Charge the bigger tenants a base rent plus a share of their sales, so lease income rises as visitor numbers grow.
4. **Give existing vendors and families who are resettled first claim on the kiosks**, as Marina did with 60% of its carts. This lowers resistance to clearing encroachments.
5. **Navigability is the biggest unknown.** Low bridges, depth and silting may limit boats to a few stretches. This needs a survey before committing to a fleet.

## For the build (feeds apps and monitoring)

Each benefit line maps to a metric the monitoring system can track. Property: guideline value and listing price per sq ft by ward. Flood: waterlogging incidents and channel depth. Health: ward-level disease cases. Spending: visitor counts. Water quality (BOD, DO, coliform) is the leading indicator that unlocks all of these.
