# Cooum: river mouth sand bar, a dam or barrage, and power (Research track 2)

_Last updated 24 Nov 2026. ₹ figures in crore. Figures marked **(est.)** are my own back-of-envelope numbers, not from a source._

## Bottom line

1. **Nobody blocks the mouth on purpose.** Sand piles up naturally. Waves push sand north along the Chennai coast, and the Cooum has almost no flow outside the monsoon, so it can't push the sand back out. Chennai Port's breakwaters make it worse, because they trap that northward sand just south of the port, which is where the Cooum meets the sea. The government digs the mouth open with machines again and again.
2. **A dam or embankment across the mouth: not feasible, and it would make things worse.** It would block flood water, which is what drowned the city in 2015. It would also stop the tide from flushing the river, and a still freshwater pond in the city with little inflow goes stagnant again.
2b. **Storing flood water for farming with adjustable sluice gates (Kid's idea) works, but upstream.** The farms (about 6,630 ha) are 40 to 70 km inland, in Thiruvallur and Kancheepuram. A mouth barrage would hold only about 3% of one season's farm demand, and in the city. Gated check dams upstream do the same job in the right place. See section 2b.
3. **What works instead:** a pair of **training walls** at the mouth (the government already proposes this, at about **₹70 cr**), some dredging maintenance, and more base flow from treated sewage water released upstream. Low **inflatable rubber weirs** further upstream are the only "mini dam" that makes sense. They lie flat during floods and hold water for boating and groundwater the rest of the year.
4. **Power: not worth building for.** A barrage would give about **0.1 to 0.3 GWh a year (est.)**, because the river is flat and the tide is small. Solar panels on the embankments and walkways could give about **14 GWh a year (est.)**, around 50 to 100 times more, for about ₹50 to 60 cr.

5. **Best low-cost, practical package (section 5):** restore tanks, recharge wells, shutters on check dams, training walls, treated water as base flow, and bank solar. About **₹180 to 250 cr** over 4 years.

## 1. Why the mouth is blocked by sand

- **Natural cause.** On the Coromandel coast, waves carry sand along the shore. Over a year, the net movement is **northward**. River mouths act as "sediment sinks" where that sand settles. Without monsoon flow there is very little tidal flushing at the Cooum, so the bar closes. ([IIT Madras Tech Talk, 2021](https://tech-talk.iitm.ac.in/?p=1154))
- **Human cause.** Chennai Port's breakwaters trap the northward drift. Satellite data from 2000 to 2019 show about **5.6 km of the Marina and Cooum shore growing (accreting)**, and the study credits the port. ([IIT Madras](https://tech-talk.iitm.ac.in/?p=1154)) The Water Resources Department also cites harbour activity as speeding up sedimentation. ([The Hindu via inkl](https://www.inkl.com/news/plan-to-build-training-wall-at-mouth-of-cooum-to-prevent-sand-bar)) Ennore shows the same pattern: port construction is blamed for closing one of the creek mouths there. ([Wikipedia, Kamarajar Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ennore_Port))
- **Low river flow.** Outside the monsoon the Cooum carries mostly sewage and very little real flow. Sewage diversion will reduce that flow further, so **cleaning the sewage alone will make the sand bar problem worse unless clean water is put back.**
- **What is done now.** The Public Works Department and Water Resources Department dredge the mouth repeatedly. Recently, machines kept it open up to **120 m** wide. ([inkl](https://www.inkl.com/news/plan-to-build-training-wall-at-mouth-of-cooum-to-prevent-sand-bar))
- **Is any of it deliberate?** I found no evidence that anyone closes the mouth on purpose. The real problem is neglect: the mouth is left to close between dredging rounds.

## 2. Options assessed

| Option | What it is | Feasible? | Cost | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Training walls at the mouth** | Two rock and tetrapod walls that jut into the sea and keep the channel open: 265 m on the north side and 310 m on the south, designed by NIOT | Yes. Needs environmental (Coastal Regulation Zone) clearance and about 18 months to build | **₹70 cr** (WRD estimate) | **Do it.** Earlier Cooum walls failed because their angle and height were wrong ([TNM, 2020](https://www.thenewsminute.com/tamil-nadu/iit-m-team-proposes-straight-training-walls-ennore-creek-reduce-flood-risk-133064)). The IIT Madras team prefers straight walls plus transitional groynes, so get that design right. |
| **Fixed dam or embankment across the mouth** | A wall that holds river water back from the sea | No | n/a | **Reject.** It blocks floods. The Cooum carried about **13,000 cusecs (about 370 m³/s)** in a recent storm ([inkl](https://www.inkl.com/news/plan-to-build-training-wall-at-mouth-of-cooum-to-prevent-sand-bar)), and the city flooded in 2015 because water couldn't get out. |
| **Gated tidal regulator or barrage near the mouth** | Gates that shut out the sea and open during floods | Technically yes | ₹150 to 300 cr **(est.)** | **Not recommended.** Gates must be fully open in every flood, so they would hold back only a small amount of water. Shutting out the tide removes the river's best natural cleaner. The barrage would also trap sand against itself, and its gates become a single point of failure in a cyclone. |
| **Inflatable rubber weirs upstream** (e.g. 2 to 4 weirs, about 1 to 1.5 m high, in the city stretch) | Low rubber dams that are inflated in the dry season and deflated during floods | Yes, a proven technology | ₹10 to 30 cr each **(est.)** | **Worth a pilot** once the water is clean. They keep a navigable, good-looking water level and recharge groundwater. Without enough clean inflow, though, they create stagnant pools, so they come after the base flow work. |
| **Upstream check dams and treated water release** | Check dams (one exists at Paruthipattu) plus releasing treated sewage water into the river | Yes | Part of the main restoration budget | **Essential.** Steady flow is what keeps the mouth open and the water fresh. |

### Side effects to plan for

- **Saltwater.** Sea water entering the lower few km is natural for an estuary and good for flushing. A barrage would cut off that saltwater, but mangroves and estuary fish depend on it.
- **Ecology.** Training walls change where sand lands. The beach just north of the mouth may erode and need sand bypassing or nourishment. This is the "terminal groyne" effect.
- **Coastal Regulation Zone (CRZ).** The mouth is a coastal regulation zone, so any structure needs a CRZ and environmental clearance. The WRD plan already counts on this.

## 2b. Kid's idea: a gated barrage that stores flood water for farming

**The idea is sound engineering.** Gated barrages are built exactly this way. The gates stay shut to store water, and they open to let excess flood water pass. India has many of them, for example the Adhanur barrage on the Kollidam: a low-head diversion structure storing 0.334 TMC for 9,159 ha of farmland, designed to pass 4.56 lakh cusecs ([TNPCB draft EIA](https://tnpcb.gov.in/pdf/ph/EIARptEngConBarrage13924.pdf)). The real question is **where** to put it on the Cooum.

### Where the farms actually are

- The Cooum sub-basin has **80 irrigation tanks with about 9,940 ha of registered farmland**. The **61 rural tanks irrigate about 6,630 ha**, all in the **upper zone**: Kancheepuram, Thiruvallur and a little of Vellore (Sriperumbudur, Walajabad, Kadambathur, Thiruvallur blocks). These are **40 to 70 km upstream** of the mouth. The middle zone is about 80% urbanised. ([IAMWARM Cooum DPR](https://iamwarm.gov.in/IAMWARM/OLD/dpr-pdf/PhaseIV/Cooum.pdf))
- The last ~20 km through Chennai has **no farmland**. Water stored at the mouth would have to be pumped 40+ km uphill to reach any farm.

### How much a mouth barrage could store vs what farms need (est.)

| | Volume |
|---|---|
| City channel held up by a gated barrage at the mouth (~20 km × ~60 m wide × ~2 m deep) | **~2.4 million m³ (~85 mcft)** |
| What the 5 existing upstream check dams already held after one good monsoon | **≥130 mcft** ([The Hindu via inkl](https://www.inkl.com/news/heavy-rainfall-fills-check-dams-across-cooum-helps-clean-polluted-stretch)) |
| Chembarambakkam lake, for scale | 3,645 mcft |
| One season of irrigation for the 6,630 ha upstream (~1.2 m of water for paddy) | **~80 million m³ (~2,800 mcft)** |

So a mouth barrage holds about **3% of one season's farm demand**, in the wrong place, in the most polluted stretch.

### Other trade-offs at the mouth

- **Flood operation.** The gates would have to be fully open before every big storm. The channel is built for about **25,000 cusecs** ([inkl](https://www.inkl.com/news/heavy-rainfall-fills-check-dams-across-cooum-helps-clean-polluted-stretch)), and Chennai's 2015 disaster was partly an operations failure: releases from Chembarambakkam came too late and too fast ([CAG](https://cag.gov.in/uploads/download_audit_report/2016/Executive_summary_of_Report_No_4_of_2017_-_Performance_Audit_of_Flood_Management_and_Response_in_Chennai_.pdf)). A gate stuck shut during a cyclone, with a storm surge on the sea side, would flood central Chennai.
- **Saltwater exclusion: a real benefit, but a small one.** Keeping the sea out would protect shallow groundwater near the mouth. But the land there is city, served by piped water, not farms.
- **Sediment.** Sea sand would pile against the seaward face and river silt behind the gates. Both mean dredging, on top of the mouth dredging already needed.
- **Water quality.** Still water in the city stretch, with low dry-season inflow, is exactly how the Cooum became a sewer. The Netherlands closed its Haringvliet estuary with sluice gates in 1970 for flood safety and fresh water. It caused fish and water-quality losses, and in 2018 the Dutch began opening the gates partly to let the tide back in (the "Kierbesluit").
- **Cost (est.).** A gated barrage on the open coast, with cyclone-rated gates: ₹150 to 300 cr. An upstream gated check dam: ₹5 to 8 cr each.

### Verdict on Kid's idea: yes to gates and storage, but upstream, not at the mouth

Store flood water **where the farms are**, with **gated check dams** in the Thiruvallur and Kancheepuram stretch. The state is already doing this without gates: Pudhumavilangai (₹7.8 cr, 2.3 m high, 100 m wide, built to irrigate Kadambathur, Satharai and Agaram and feed nearby lakes; [DT Next](https://www.dtnext.in/amp/story/news/tamilnadu/unpolluted-part-of-cooum-to-get-5th-check-dam)) and Soranjeri (₹5.62 cr sanctioned in 2022; [WRD G.O. 12](https://wrd.tn.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/SPID-1321-G.O-2D-No.12-WR-S1-Dept-dated-23.02.2022-1.pdf)). Adding **adjustable shutters** to these dams is Kid's idea applied in the right place: hold more water after the monsoon, open fully in floods, and release it slowly to recharge groundwater and keep base flow going to the city. That base flow is also what keeps the mouth open.

The best combined layout:
1. **Upstream (40 to 70 km): gated check dams + desilted, linked irrigation tanks.** This is the storage for farming. Together the 80 tanks can hold far more than any single barrage.
2. **City stretch: 2 to 4 low inflatable or gated weirs**, for boating, appearance and recharge, deflated or opened in floods.
3. **Mouth: training walls**, with no gates, so floods always have a free exit and the tide keeps flushing the river.

## 3. Power generation, with numbers

Power from water is **P = ρ·g·Q·H·η**: water density × gravity × flow × height drop × efficiency.

**a) Run-of-river at a weir or barrage (est.)**
- Dry season clean flow, assuming treated water is returned: about **3 m³/s** (an assumption, not a measured figure).
- Usable head: **1 to 1.5 m** (the city stretch is almost flat).
- P = 1000 × 9.81 × 3 × 1.5 × 0.7 ≈ **31 kW**, or about **0.27 GWh a year** at best.
- During floods the gates must be open, so the head drops to zero just when flow is high.

**b) Tidal barrage at the mouth (est.)**
- Chennai's tidal range is about **1.2 m at maximum spring tide**, and about 1 m used in models ([IJST, 2016](https://indjst.org/articles/theoretical-prediction-of-annual-energy-generation-from-a-tidal-barrage-with-reference-to-tidal-range-at-chennai)). Working tidal plants need ranges of 5 m or more.
- Energy per tide = ½·ρ·g·A·R². The Cooum's backwater area is about 0.3 km² (an assumption). That gives about 0.4 MWh per tide, or **about 0.1 GWh a year** after realistic losses.
- The IJST paper's best case for the whole Chennai coast is only about 10 to 20 GWh a year, and that assumes a basin much larger than the Cooum.

**c) Solar on embankments, walkways and canopies (est.)**
- 10 km of riverfront, with a 5 m strip on each bank, is about 10 ha. That fits about **10 MWp**.
- At about 1,400 kWh per kWp per year in Chennai, that is about **14 GWh a year**, enough for about 10,000 homes.
- Cost: about **₹50 to 60 cr** at ₹5 to 6 cr/MWp.
- Floating solar *on* the channel is not recommended. The panels would sit in the flood path and slow the water down.

| Source | Energy per year | Capital cost | Comment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weir hydro | ~0.27 GWh | High per kW | Too small to matter |
| Tidal barrage | ~0.1 GWh | ₹150 cr+ | Tide is too small |
| **Embankment solar** | **~14 GWh** | ~₹55 cr | **Best option.** Can also light and shade the riverfront |

## 4. Verdict

- **Do not dam the mouth.** Build the training walls (₹70 cr). Keep the mouth open by returning treated water upstream and by occasional dredging.
- **Optional "mini dams":** inflatable rubber weirs upstream, only after the water is clean and has steady flow.
- **Power:** skip hydro and tidal. Use solar along the embankments, at about 50 to 100 times more energy for the money.

## 5. All options compared: lowest cost, highest yield, practical

Kid's brief: explore every option, keep cost low, get the most out of it, and keep it practical. "Practical" here means three things: already proven in Tamil Nadu, buildable by agencies that exist today (WRD, Metrowater, farmer tank associations), and easy to maintain.

**Yield per ₹1 cr** puts the options on one scale: mcft of water stored or recharged, or GWh of power per year. Figures marked (est.) are my own estimates.

### 5d. Recommended combination for us

Cheapest and most proven first. Each phase pays off without waiting for the next.

| Phase | What | Cost (est.) | What we get |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Year 0 to 1** | Restore the 80 Cooum tanks and their channels; build 300 to 500 recharge wells; add shutters to the existing check dams; keep dredging the mouth | **₹30 to 50 cr** | Water for ~6,600 to 9,900 ha of farmland, recharged groundwater, smaller floods |
| **2. Year 1 to 3** | Training walls at the mouth; 2 to 3 more shuttered check dams (Soranjeri is already sanctioned); first 5 MWp of bank solar | **₹110 to 140 cr** | A mouth that stays open year-round, more stored water, ~7 GWh a year of power |
| **3. Year 2 to 4** | Release treated water from Koyambedu as base flow (alongside sewage cleanup); pilot 1 to 2 city weirs (rubber or gated); expand solar to 10 MWp | **₹40 to 60 cr**, plus the sewage programme | A river that flows year-round in the city, boating stretches, ~14 GWh a year |
| **Total** | | **~₹180 to 250 cr** | About the cost of one mouth barrage, but with far more water, farmland served, flood protection and power |

**Skip:** the mouth barrage, tidal power, micro-hydro, floating solar on the river channel, and a new reservoir.

## Learnings needed (for the project's learning list)

- Coastal sediment transport: littoral drift, how sand bars form, how training walls and groynes work
- Basic hydraulics: flow, head, the hydro power equation, flood discharge
- Estuary ecology: saltwater, mangroves, tidal flushing
- CRZ Notification 2019 and environmental clearance steps
- Rubber dam and barrage design basics, and how to size and cost solar
